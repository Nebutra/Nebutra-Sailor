## "What does it cost to build a commercial product on this?"

**Nothing.**

No fee, no licence key, no registration, no revenue threshold, no headcount
threshold, no copyleft obligation. Build a closed-source product, sell it,
keep the money.

If you previously read that you needed an Independent Developer License, a
Single-Owner Company ("OPC") License or a $799/year Startup License, that is no
longer true — those tiers were retired on 2026-07-26. If you read that the
repository is FSL with a competing-use restriction, that ended on 2026-10-16.
See [License History](./license-history.md).

## "Then what am I actually bound by?"

Apache-2.0's conditions apply only when you **redistribute** Sailor's source,
or a derivative work of it, in source or object form (section 4):

1. Give recipients a copy of the Apache-2.0 licence.
2. Mark files you modified with a prominent notice that you changed them.
3. Keep the copyright, patent, trademark and attribution notices in the source
   you redistribute.
4. Carry the [`NOTICE`](../../NOTICE) file's attribution notices along — in a
   `NOTICE` file you distribute, in your documentation, or where third-party
   notices normally appear. You may add your own notices next to ours.

Running a hosted product built on Sailor is not redistributing its source.
Projects generated by `create-sailor` already ship the `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`
these conditions refer to; keep them if you publish or hand over the source.

## "Is there anything I can't do?"

Two things, both standard Apache-2.0:

- **Use the Nebutra or Sailor trademarks** to name or market your product.
  Apache-2.0 grants no trademark rights (section 6). Rebranding Sailor under
  your own, unrelated name needs no permission. See
  [TRADEMARK.md](../../TRADEMARK.md).
- **Keep the patent licence while suing over it.** If you start patent
  litigation alleging that Sailor infringes a patent, the patent licence
  Apache-2.0 granted you for Sailor ends (section 3).

There is no competing-use clause. Offering a hosted or repackaged platform
built from Sailor is allowed.

## "Do I have to say 'Built with Nebutra-Sailor'?"

**No.** Attribution in your product UI or marketing is appreciated and never
required. The only attribution Apache-2.0 asks for is keeping the `NOTICE`
and copyright notices when you redistribute the source (see above).

## "Can I use it for client work / agency projects?"

**Yes.** Build client projects on Sailor and charge whatever you like. If you
hand the source to the client, include the `LICENSE` and `NOTICE`.

## "We're a 5,000-person company. Do we need a licence?"

**Not to use it.** Headcount and revenue are irrelevant.

You may still want an Enterprise agreement, for different reasons: indemnity,
a contractual SLA, a signed DPA, compliance documentation, trademark rights,
or a named counterparty your procurement process can point at. That is buying
assurance, not permission. See [Commercial Support](./commercial-support.md).

## "Can I fork it and keep my changes private?"

Yes. Apache-2.0 has no copyleft and no network-use trigger. There is no
obligation to publish modifications. If you *redistribute the source* of your
fork, the section 4 conditions above travel with it.

## "What about versions I already have under FSL, MIT or AGPL?"

Versions released before 2026-10-16 remain available under the licence they
shipped with — FSL-1.1-ALv2 for the repository (2026-07-26 onward), MIT for
npm packages, AGPL-3.0-only for the repository before 2026-07-26. Those grants
were irrevocable. Pulling any version from 2026-10-16 onward puts you on
Apache-2.0.

## "Can I contribute while using it commercially?"

Yes. Contributions are accepted under Apache-2.0 (section 5: inbound =
outbound). Substantial contributions may additionally be asked to sign the
[CLA](./CLA.md). You retain copyright. Contributing changes nothing about your
rights as a user.

---

## "Why Apache-2.0 rather than MIT?"

Both are permissive. Apache-2.0 adds an explicit patent grant from every
contributor and a `NOTICE` mechanism for attribution, which suits a codebase
companies build products on.
